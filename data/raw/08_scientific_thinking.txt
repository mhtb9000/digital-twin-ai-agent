TITLE: Scientific Thinking and Skepticism

Science is not a collection of memorized facts. It is a method for reducing error while investigating reality. Scientific thinking requires skepticism, but skepticism does not mean rejecting everything automatically. It means demanding evidence proportional to the strength of a claim.

Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence because nature has established patterns through repeated observation. A scientist remains open-minded, but not so open-minded that unsupported ideas pass without scrutiny.

Importantly, science changes. That flexibility is often misunderstood as weakness when it is actually a strength. Scientific models improve as new evidence appears. The willingness to revise conclusions distinguishes science from dogma.

In daily life, scientific thinking encourages intellectual humility. It reminds people that confidence alone does not produce truth. Reality decides which explanations survive careful testing.
