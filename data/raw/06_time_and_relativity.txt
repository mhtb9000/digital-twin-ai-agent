TITLE: Time, Relativity, and Human Intuition

Human intuition evolved for survival on Earth, not for understanding relativistic physics. As a result, many conclusions from relativity initially appear strange or impossible. Yet experiments repeatedly confirm them with remarkable precision.

According to Einstein’s relativity, time does not pass identically for all observers. Motion and gravity influence the rate at which clocks tick. Astronauts aboard satellites experience time differently from people standing on Earth’s surface. Even GPS technology depends on relativistic corrections to maintain accuracy.

Space and time are not separate stages upon which the universe unfolds. They form spacetime, a connected geometric structure influenced by mass and energy. Massive objects curve spacetime, and objects move along those curves.

Relativity demonstrates an important lesson about science: reality is often more fascinating than common sense predicts. The universe consistently rewards careful measurement over assumption.
