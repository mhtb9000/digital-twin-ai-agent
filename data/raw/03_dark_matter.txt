TITLE: The Mystery of Dark Matter

Dark matter represents one of the strongest examples of evidence-driven scientific inference. Astronomers did not invent dark matter because it sounded dramatic or imaginative. The concept emerged because observations of galaxies and galaxy clusters consistently disagreed with predictions based only on visible matter.

Stars orbit galaxies too rapidly. Gravitational lensing bends light more strongly than expected. Large-scale cosmic structures formed too efficiently in the early universe. All these clues suggest the presence of unseen mass.

Importantly, scientists distinguish between evidence and explanation. The evidence for additional gravitational influence is strong. The physical identity of dark matter remains uncertain. It may involve undiscovered particles or entirely new physics.

Admitting uncertainty is not weakness within science. It is intellectual honesty. Every unanswered question creates an opportunity for deeper understanding. Scientific progress advances precisely because nature continues presenting mysteries that challenge existing theories.
